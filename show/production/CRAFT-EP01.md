# Craft extraction — the Jack/Primal method applied to Mochiichao EP01

EP01's confirmed shape (owner, 2026-10-27): Mochiko-focused; the
world's creatures and scenery and the life within them carry the
episode; not very verbal; few humans; Hex and Aeon appear as CAMEOS
only. This is a Samurai Jack episode in structure — and, under
Mochiichao's own laws (creatures = pure cries, subtitles rationed to
earned moments), it sits even closer to Primal. Below: each Jack/Primal
mechanic, then where it lands in EP01's beat map (locked 28 cuts =
spine S1–S5 + stinger; expansion clusters N1–N10).

## 1. Environment gets real minutes, first
Jack method: "a minute or two sequence of just establishing the
environment… you really feel that desert."
→ **N1 "Dawn Before the Keeper" (78s, 6 cuts)** is already paced like
Jack — ~13s per cut before the keeper even matters. Direction: resist
any recut that shortens N1's waking beats; the Gate, mist, and first
creature life ARE the cold open. The episode's title subject is the
Garden; N1 is its portrait, shot the way Jack shoots a landscape.
→ **N10 rhymes N1 shot-for-shot in structure** — Jack bookends places
the same way (a location re-seen after the story has changed its
meaning). Protect the rhyme in the edit; it is the episode's frame.

## 2. One sense is the episode's channel: hearing
Blind Archers doctrine: declare a sense, route every beat through it.
EP01's channel is already hearing — the Forest that *hums*, the
Garden's morning bed, the stinger's engineered absence (−51.9 LUFS by
design). Concretes:
- **The sweat-drop rule** (Four Seasons "Fall"): one tiny sound may
  carry a plot turn. EP01's cold-spot shimmer is that beat — mix it
  exposed, nothing under it, the way the droplet lands on the leaf.
- **N9 "Night in the Garden" (104s) and its 10-second held silence**:
  in Jack terms 10s is a *short* hold — Jack holds run to minutes.
  Treat the 10s as a floor, not a ceiling: if the night bed is
  working, let the hold breathe in the final mix rather than trimming
  to the map. Silence in Jack is never empty — it is the room tone of
  the place (wind state, distant cries, the Forest NOT humming).
- **N7 "The Stakeout Builds"**: light dies in real time across the
  cluster. Shoot/mix it as one slow movement (Jack's "slow" in
  fight-fight-fight-slow): sound thins as light thins, in parallel.

## 3. Rhythm, not coverage
Action and work alike are composed "fast-fast-fast-slow."
→ **N6 "The Garden at Work" (84s)** and the rounds clusters **N2/N3**
are process montages — the Four Seasons "Winter" lesson: watching
skilled work done well, at length, is content. Cut the rounds on a
rhythm (task, task, task… hold) rather than on information; the
audience should be able to feel the Garden's tempo by N6.
→ The locked spine's gag beats (pyramid stacking/un-stacking) play as
the "slow" movements between work rhythms — don't speed them up to
feel like progress.

## 4. Creature life carries meaning (the Primal layer)
- **Pose and eyes, not gesture**: the six rigs' idle/performance
  animation is EP01's acting. Prioritise eye-line and posture beats in
  N2/N3/N8 over extra motion — a creature *watching* Mochiko work is a
  complete beat in this grammar.
- **Bond by mirrored deeds**: Primal builds Spear/Fang from parallel
  loss and shared tasks. EP01's Mochiko/Wispkin line is the same
  machine: both keep rounds (N2 hers, N8/N9 his). Compose the rhyme —
  same jobs, same routes, day/night — so the audience assembles the
  relationship before any subtitle names it. Intertitle 4 ("…This is
  my round. Every night.") then works like the Blind Archers' coda:
  three quiet lines after long silence, carrying the whole verdict.
- **Per-creature sound palettes**: Fang's clicks-and-moans doctrine =
  design each pilot species' EP01 cry palette as an asset, not a
  default. Wispkin's near-silence is itself a palette choice — the
  ghost is the quietest thing in a sound-led episode, which is why the
  stinger works.

## 5. Cameos, the Jack way
Jack's world is full of figures who pass through at distance and are
gone; presence is rationed. EP01's two cameos map cleanly:
- **Hex** — the beat map's proposal (distant silhouette at the far
  fence during N1 or N10, gone when looked at directly) IS a Jack
  shot: an unexplained figure at the edge of a held landscape frame.
  No line, no approach, no name spoken. If used in N1, it bookends
  with N10's rhyme.
- **Aeon** — presence as signal, not person: the device-only ping (an
  Aku's-eye-style intrusion of the knowing world into the quiet one).
  One sound, once — the episode's only "technology" note, and it
  should feel like it comes from outside the Garden's sound-world.
Both cameos protect the law of the episode: the Garden belongs to
Mochiko and the creatures; the wider human story is weather on the
 horizon.

## 6. Color keys for light states (Wills method → plate discipline)
Wills painted a color key for every shot whose light mattered and
made the production match the key exactly. EP01's production map
already names the same objects as GAPs: Gate dawn grade variant,
living Pond plate (day), Pond dusk, Gate night. Treat each light state
(dawn / day / dusk / night) as a KEY: one graded master per location
per state, and every composite in that state matches its key — the
grade variants stop being "variants" and become the keys the episode
is painted from. This is the cheapest available jump in EP01's visual
authority, and it is exactly how Jack got feature-looking episodes on
a TV schedule.

## 7. What NOT to import
- Jack's quest frame (a lone warrior crossing a hostile world) — EP01
  is the inverse: a keeper at home in a loved place. Borrow the
  grammar (holds, sound, environment), not the loneliness.
- Primal's brutality register — Mochiichao keeps its own tone; the
  borrow is the silence and the creature-truth, not the gore.
- Any Jack/Primal character, name, design, or music — construction
  kit only, per the standing rule.
